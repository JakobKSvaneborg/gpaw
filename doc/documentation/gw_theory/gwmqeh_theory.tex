\begin{abstract}
We document the mathematics behind \texttt{gpaw.response.gwqeh.GWmQEHCorrection},
which extends the $G\Delta W$ method of Winther and
Thygesen [2D~Mater.~\textbf{4}, 025059 (2017)] from the monopole approximation
to the full multi-pole quantum-electrostatic heterostructure (mQEH)
basis.  The original $G\Delta W$ method assumes that the change in the
screened Coulomb interaction $\Delta W^i$ of a 2D layer $i$ embedded
in a van der Waals heterostructure is well described by its in-plane
macroscopic ($\GG=\GG'=0$) component, equivalent to keeping a single
monopole density basis function per layer.  In the mQEH generalisation
the building block of each layer carries multiple density basis
functions $\{\rho_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{N_i}$, and the QEH library
returns the matrix
\[
W^{\text{mQEH}}_{\alpha\beta}(q_\parallel,\omega) =
\int\!\dd z\,\dd z'\;\rho_{\alpha}^{*}(z;q_\parallel)\;
W^{\Heter}(z,z';q_\parallel,\omega)\;\rho_{\beta}(z';q_\parallel)
\]
on a heterostructure $z$-grid.  Each GPAW Bloch pair density
$\bar\rho^{nm}(\Gpar;z)$, after Fourier-transforming the out-of-plane
plane-wave index $\Gz$ into $z$, is then \emph{least-squares-fitted}
into the (non-orthogonal) density basis to extract expansion
coefficients $d^{nm}_{\alpha}(\qpar+\Gpar)$.  The mQEH self-energy
contribution is the bilinear form
$\sum_{\alpha\beta} d^{nm*}_{\alpha} W^{\text{mQEH}}_{\alpha\beta}
d^{nm}_{\beta}$ summed over in-plane $\Gpar$ shells and over band
transitions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Recap of the $G\Delta W$ method}

The screened Coulomb interaction
\begin{equation}
W(\rr,\rr',\omega) = \int \dd\rr''\, \eps^{-1}(\rr,\rr'',\omega)\,
   V(\rr'',\rr'),
\label{eq:W-def}
\end{equation}
splits in a heterostructure of 2D layers as
\begin{equation}
W^{i,\Heter}(\rr,\rr',\omega) =
   W^i(\rr,\rr',\omega) + \Delta W^i(\rr,\rr',\omega),
\label{eq:dW-decomp}
\end{equation}
where $W^i$ is the freestanding-monolayer screened interaction and
$\Delta W^i$ is the change in screening due to the surrounding layers
(Eq.~(5) of the paper).  The QP correction due to the environment is
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{n\kk}^{i,\text{scr}} = Z_{n\kk}^i\,
   \mathrm{Re}\,\Delta\Sigma_{n\kk}^i(\omega=\eps_{n\kk}),
\label{eq:dE-Z-dSigma}
\end{equation}
with $Z_{n\kk}^i = (1 - \partial_\omega\Sigma_{n\kk})^{-1}$ the GW
renormalisation factor (Eq.~(6) of the paper).

In a plane-wave representation the full GW self-energy on top of
DFT eigenstates reads
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{n\kk}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\!\int_\text{BZ}\!\dd\qq\,
\frac{\im}{2\pi}\!\int\dd\omega'\sum_{m,\GG\GG'}
\frac{\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\GG)\,
      \bigl[\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\GG')\bigr]^{*}}
     {\omega+\omega'-\eps_{m\kk+\qq}-\im\eta\,\sgn(\eps_{m\kk+\qq}-\mu)}
\,\widetilde W_{\GG\GG'}(\qq,\omega'),
\label{eq:Sigma-PW}
\end{equation}
with $\widetilde W = W - V$ (Eq.~(9) of the paper) and the pair density
\begin{equation}
\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\GG) =
   \braket{n\kk | \, e^{\im (\qq+\GG)\!\cdot\!\rr}\,| m,\kk+\qq}.
\label{eq:pair-density}
\end{equation}
Both the paper and the implementation use the $\kk+\qq$ convention:
the inner k-point in \texttt{calculate\_QEH} is obtained via
\texttt{K2 = kd.find\_k\_plus\_q(Q\_c, [kpt1.K])} so that
$\kk_2 = \kk_1+\qq$, and \texttt{deps\_m = eps1 - kpt2.eps\_n} encodes
$\eps_{n\kk}-\eps_{m,\kk+\qq}$ (\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1145, 1196).
The $e^{+\im(\qq+\GG)\cdot\rr}$ matrix element in \eq{pair-density}
is consistent with momentum conservation $\kk_2 = \kk+\qq$ modulo a
reciprocal-lattice vector $\GG$.

\paragraph{Original (monopole) approximation.}
$\Delta W^i$ is generated by charges \emph{outside} layer~$i$ and
therefore varies slowly across the (thin) layer.  Winther \& Thygesen
exploit this to retain only the macroscopic component
$\GG=\GG'=0$ in \eq{Sigma-PW}, which is the average of
$\Delta W^i(z,z',\qq,\omega)$ over the layer thickness $d_i$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta W^i(\qq,\omega) =
\frac{1}{d_i^2}\int_{z_i-d_i/2}^{z_i+d_i/2}\!\!\!\!\int
W^{\Heter}(z,z',\qq,\omega) - W^i(z,z',\qq,\omega)\,\dd z\,\dd z'.
\label{eq:dW-monopole}
\end{equation}
The resulting self-energy correction is the central equation of the
original $G\Delta W$ paper (its Eq.~(12)),
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Sigma_{n\kk}^{i,\text{mono}}\;=\;
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\!\int_\text{BZ}\!\!\dd\qq\,
\frac{\im}{2\pi}\!\int\dd\omega'\sum_{m}
\frac{|\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\GG=0)|^{2}}
     {\omega+\omega'-\eps_{m\kk+\qq}-\im\eta\,\sgn(\eps_{m\kk+\qq}-\mu)}
\,\Delta W^i(\qq,\omega').
\label{eq:Sigma-monopole}
\end{equation}
This is the formula implemented in the parent class
\texttt{GWQEHCorrection.calculate\_QEH} of
\texttt{gpaw/response/gwqeh.py}: the descriptor is built with
\texttt{self.ecut = 0.1\,Ha} so that the plane-wave basis contains
exactly the single vector $\GG=0$.

\section{The mQEH density basis and \texorpdfstring{$W^{\text{mQEH}}$}{W\_mQEH}}

\subsection{Density basis functions}

The QEH model represents the linear density response of an isolated
2D layer~$i$ in a small basis of \emph{density basis functions}
$\{\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(z;q_\parallel)\}_{\alpha=1}^{N_i}$, which depend
on the magnitude of the in-plane momentum
$q_\parallel = |\qpar|$ (see
\texttt{qeh/bb\_calculator/basis\_functions.py::MQEHBasis} for the
construction as eigenvectors of $V\chi$).  The original monopole QEH
corresponds to $N_i=1$ with $\rho^{i}_{1}$ the constant in-plane
charge profile of the layer.

It is essential for what follows that the \texttt{Layer} class only
ships the density basis to the consumer through
\texttt{get\_drho\_qza} (\texttt{qeh/buildingblock.py}\,L.~525--526).
A potential basis $\phi^{i}_{\alpha}(z;q_\parallel)$ is also defined
internally, but it is hard-zeroed outside the geometric layer width
in \texttt{Layer.get\_phi\_qaz} (\texttt{qeh/buildingblock.py}\,L.~535--548),
\emph{breaking} the biorthogonality
$\int\dd z\,\phi^{i*}_{\alpha}\rho^{i}_{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$
that holds on the building-block grid but \emph{not} on the
heterostructure grid that the mQEH self-energy actually integrates
over.  The implementation therefore deliberately \emph{never reaches
for $\phi$}; the docstring of \texttt{GWmQEHCorrection.\_\_init\_\_}
contains an explicit warning to this effect
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~723--730).  Section~\ref{sec:LS} below explains
how the loss of biorthogonality is replaced by a least-squares
projection in the density basis alone.

\subsection{The mQEH $W$ matrix}

In the density basis the screened interaction of a whole
heterostructure becomes an $N\times N$ matrix, with
$N = \sum_{l} N_l$ summing the basis sizes of every layer.  The QEH
library produces it as
\begin{equation}
\hat W^{\Heter}(q_\parallel,\omega)
\;=\; \hat V(q_\parallel) \;+\;
\hat V(q_\parallel)\,\hat\chi^{\Heter}(q_\parallel,\omega)\,
\hat V(q_\parallel),
\label{eq:W-matrix}
\end{equation}
where each entry of $\hat W$ is taken to be the symmetric
$\langle\rho|W|\rho\rangle$ matrix element,
\begin{equation}
W^{\text{mQEH}}_{(i\alpha)(j\beta)}(q_\parallel,\omega)
\;=\; \int\!\dd z\,\dd z'\;
\rho^{i*}_{\alpha}(z;q_\parallel)\;
W^{\Heter}(z,z';q_\parallel,\omega)\;
\rho^{j}_{\beta}(z';q_\parallel),
\label{eq:WmQEH-def}
\end{equation}
i.e., $\hat W^{\text{mQEH}}$ takes the rho-coefficients of an external
probe density in and returns rho-projections of the resulting
screened potential out (the comment block at \texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1307--1311
makes this explicit).  The change in screening acting on layer $i$ is
the on-layer block of the difference between full heterostructure and
isolated monolayer,
\begin{equation}
\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}(q_\parallel,\omega)\;=\;
W^{\Heter}_{(i\alpha)(i\beta)}(q_\parallel,\omega)
\;-\;W^{i,0}_{\alpha\beta}(q_\parallel,\omega),
\qquad \alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,N_i,
\label{eq:dW-matrix}
\end{equation}
sliced in \texttt{calculate\_W\_QEH} (\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~895--904).
The bare Coulomb contribution $\hat V$ of \eq{W-matrix} cancels
identically in \eq{dW-matrix}, so the implementation calls
\texttt{get\_screened\_potential(subtract\_bare\_coulomb=True)} on
both sides (\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~883, 892), avoiding a wasted
matrix-add and -subtract pair.  This is a pure efficiency change; the
underlying math is unaltered.

\section{Self-energy in the mQEH basis}

\subsection{In-plane / out-of-plane split and the relevant integrand}

Decompose each plane-wave vector as $\GG=(\Gpar,\Gz)$ and write the
in-plane reciprocal vector $\qpar+\Gpar$.  By in-plane translational
invariance of the heterostructure $\Delta W^i$ is diagonal in
$\Gpar$, so the double PW sum in \eq{Sigma-PW} contracts to
\begin{multline}
\sum_{\GG\GG'}\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\GG)\,
\bigl[\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\GG')\bigr]^{*}\,
\Delta W^{i}_{\GG\GG'}(\qq,\omega)
\;=\\
\sum_{\Gpar}
\int\!\dd z\,\dd z'\;
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z)\,
\Delta W^{i}\!\bigl(\,|\qpar+\Gpar|\,;\,z,z'\,\bigr)\,
\bigl[\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z')\bigr]^{*},
\label{eq:GGsum-to-zz}
\end{multline}
with the $z$-resolved in-plane Fourier component of the pair density
\begin{equation}
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z) \;=\;
\frac{1}{L_z}\sum_{\Gz}
\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar,\Gz)\, e^{+\im\Gz z}.
\label{eq:rho-z}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/L_z$ on the right of \eq{rho-z} is the inverse-FFT
normalisation that absorbs the missing $\Gz$ sum from the PW measure
$1/\Omega = 1/(AL_z)$.  The sign $e^{+\im\Gz z}$ matches the
$e^{-\im\GG\!\cdot\!\rr}$ convention used by GPAW's
\texttt{ActualPairDensityCalculator}.

\subsection{Density-basis least-squares projection of the pair density}
\label{sec:LS}

The approximation that defines mQEH lives entirely in the
\emph{pair density}, not in the screened interaction.  The matrix
$\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}(q_\parallel,\omega)$ produced by QEH
(\eq{WmQEH-def}) is the \emph{exact} matrix element of the true
kernel $\Delta W^{i}(z,z';q_\parallel,\omega)$ between
$\rho^{i}_{\alpha}$ and $\rho^{i}_{\beta}$; it is \emph{not} the
expansion coefficient of a rank-$N_i$ ansatz for $\Delta W^{i}$
itself.  Writing the kernel as
$\Delta W^{i}(z,z')=\sum_{\alpha\beta}\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(z)\,
\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\,\rho^{i*}_{\beta}(z')$ would be the
distinct (and unjustified) rank-$N_i$ replacement
$\Delta W\leftarrow S^{-1}(\Delta W)S^{-1}$ in the operator sense ---
the two coincide only when $S=\mathbb{1}$, which is generically false
on the heterostructure $z$-grid (see below).  The implementation does
\emph{not} do this; it uses the exact matrix elements as they come
out of QEH and approximates the pair density instead.

Approximate the $z$-resolved pair density in the density basis,
\begin{equation}
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z)\;\approx\;
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_i} d^{(nm)}_{\alpha}(\qpar+\Gpar)\;
\rho^{i}_{\alpha}\!\bigl(z;|\qpar+\Gpar|\bigr),
\label{eq:rho-expansion}
\end{equation}
where the coefficients are chosen to minimise the residual on the
heterostructure $z$-grid,
\begin{equation}
\{d^{(nm)}_{\alpha}\}\;=\;
\underset{\{d_\alpha\}}{\arg\min}\;
\Bigl\|\,\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;\cdot)
\;-\;\sum_{\alpha} d_{\alpha}\,\rho^{i}_{\alpha}\Bigr\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\text{het grid})}.
\label{eq:LS-variational}
\end{equation}
The orthogonal complement
\begin{equation}
\bar\rho^{\perp}_{nm}(\Gpar;z)\;=\;
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z)\;-\;
\sum_{\alpha} d^{(nm)}_{\alpha}\,\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(z;|\qpar+\Gpar|)
\label{eq:rho-perp}
\end{equation}
is what the mQEH self-energy discards; enriching $\{\rho_\alpha\}$
shrinks $\|\bar\rho^{\perp}\|$ monotonically and the result
approaches the exact PW form.

Setting $\partial/\partial d^*_\alpha\|\,\bar\rho-\sum_\beta d_\beta
\rho_\beta\|^{2}=0$ yields the normal equation
$S\,d=R$ with the rho--rho overlap and the rho--pair-density overlap
\begin{equation}
S_{\alpha\beta}(q_\parallel)\;=\;
\int\!\dd z\;\rho^{i*}_{\alpha}(z;q_\parallel)\,
\rho^{i}_{\beta}(z;q_\parallel),\qquad
R^{(nm)}_{\alpha}(\qpar+\Gpar)\;=\;
\int\!\dd z\;\rho^{i*}_{\alpha}\!\bigl(z;|\qpar+\Gpar|\bigr)\,
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z),
\label{eq:S-and-R}
\end{equation}
both on the heterostructure $z$-grid; crucially $S\neq\mathbb{1}$
because $\{\rho_\alpha\}$ is biorthogonal to the truncated
$\phi$-basis (which we cannot access on the het grid; see Sec.~5),
not orthonormal in the usual $L^{2}$ sense.  The coefficients are
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
d^{(nm)}_{\alpha}(\qpar+\Gpar)\;=\;
\sum_{\beta}\bigl[S^{-1}\bigr]_{\alpha\beta}(|\qpar+\Gpar|)\;
R^{(nm)}_{\beta}(\qpar+\Gpar).
\;}
\label{eq:d-def}
\end{equation}

Substituting \eq{rho-expansion} into the $(z,z')$ integrand of
\eq{GGsum-to-zz} and using \emph{exact} matrix elements for
$\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}$,
\begin{multline}
\int\!\dd z\,\dd z'\;
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq*}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z)\,
\Delta W^{i}(z,z';|\qpar+\Gpar|,\omega)\,
\bar\rho^{m,\kk+\qq}_{n,\kk}(\Gpar;z')
\;\approx\\
\sum_{\alpha\beta}\;
d^{(nm)*}_{\alpha}\;\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}(|\qpar+\Gpar|,\omega)\;
d^{(nm)}_{\beta},
\label{eq:GGsum-final}
\end{multline}
with the approximation error
$\int\bar\rho^{\perp*}\Delta W\bar\rho^{\perp}+\text{cross terms}$
suppressed in the rich-basis limit.  The right-hand side of
\eq{GGsum-final} is the bilinear form the implementation evaluates.
The least-squares route is precisely what the code does
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1381--1384, inside
\texttt{\_calculate\_sigma\_mqeh}):
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# R_{m,a} = int rho_a*(z) bar_rho(z) dz
R_m_a = (rho_mz @ drho_za.conj()) * self.dz_qeh
# Normal equation: S @ d.T = R.T  =>  d = solve(S, R.T).T
d_mGpar_a[:, ig, :] = np.linalg.solve(S_Gpar_ab[ig], R_m_a.T).T
\end{lstlisting}
followed by the contraction \texttt{(d\_a.conj() @ W\_ab @ d\_a)}
at L.~1402--1403.

\subsection{Final self-energy formula}

Inserting \eq{GGsum-to-zz}, \eq{rho-z}, and \eq{GGsum-final} into
\eq{Sigma-PW}, restricted to the difference $\Delta W^i$, gives
\begin{multline}
\Delta\Sigma^{i,\text{mQEH}}_{n\kk}\;=\;
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3 L_z}\!\int_\text{BZ}\!\dd\qq\;
\frac{\im}{2\pi}\!\int\dd\omega'\!\sum_{m,\Gpar}\\[2pt]
\times\;
\frac{\sum_{\alpha\beta}
   d^{(nm)*}_{\alpha}(\qpar+\Gpar)\,
   \Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\!\bigl(|\qpar+\Gpar|,\omega'\bigr)\,
   d^{(nm)}_{\beta}(\qpar+\Gpar)}
{\omega+\omega'-\eps_{m\kk+\qq}-\im\eta\,\sgn(\eps_{m\kk+\qq}-\mu)}.
\label{eq:Sigma-mQEH-final}
\end{multline}
The explicit $1/L_z$ comes from \eq{rho-z}; the remaining
$1/(2\pi)^3\!\int\dd\qq$ is the 3D BZ measure of the original PW
expression.  As shown in (M2) below, the implementation absorbs the
$L_z$ by multiplying $\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}$ by $L$ once per
$\Gpar$ (\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1158), after which the parent's
prefactor $x = 1/(N_q\,2\pi\,\Omega)$ can be reused unchanged and the
effective in-plane normalisation reduces to $1/(N_q\,2\pi\,A)$.

\subsection{Frequency representation}

As in the parent class the $\omega'$ integral is performed through a
Hilbert transform on the non-uniform spectral grid $\{\omega_w\}$.
The code stores the $\pm\im\eta$ branches in a single tensor
\texttt{Wpm\_Gpar} of shape $(n_{\Gpar},2N_\omega,N_i,N_i)$:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
Wpm_Gpar[ig, :nw] = dW_wab
Wpm_Gpar[ig, nw:] = dW_wab.copy()
self.htp(Wpm_Gpar[ig, :nw])
self.htm(Wpm_Gpar[ig, nw:])
\end{lstlisting}
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1161--1166).  After this transformation the
contribution from each electronic state $m$ reduces to a linear
interpolation between two adjacent frequency points exactly as in the
standard GW code, but with the scalar $W_w$ replaced by an
$N_i\!\times\!N_i$ matrix $W^{\pm}_{w,\alpha\beta}$.

\section{Mapping the math to \texttt{GWmQEHCorrection}}

We list the correspondence between each step in
\texttt{calculate\_QEH} / \texttt{\_calculate\_sigma\_mqeh}
(\texttt{gpaw/response/gwqeh.py}) and the equations above.

\paragraph{(M1) Building the in-plane PW basis.}
The cutoff is raised from the monopole limit
$E_\text{cut}=0.1\,\text{Ha}$ to a user-controlled value
$E_\text{cut}^{\text{mQEH}}$, exposed as
\texttt{ecut\_mqeh} (default $50\,\text{eV}$, L.~703):
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
pd0 = SingleQPWDescriptor.from_q(q_c, self.ecut_mqeh,
                                 self.gs.gd, gammacentered=True)
G_Gv  = pd0.get_reciprocal_vectors()           # q + G
G0_Gv = pd0.get_reciprocal_vectors(add_q=False)
\end{lstlisting}
The vectors are partitioned by their in-plane component
$\Gpar=(G_x,G_y)$:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
Gpar_rounded = np.round(G0_Gv[:, :2], decimals=8)
unique_Gpar, inverse_idx = np.unique(
    Gpar_rounded, axis=0, return_inverse=True)
\end{lstlisting}
This realises the partitioning of the inner sum
$\sum_{\GG\GG'}\rightarrow \sum_{\Gpar}\sum_{\Gz\Gz'}$ that leads to
\eq{GGsum-to-zz}.  An assertion on the cell metric (L.~760--764)
ensures that the third lattice vector is along Cartesian $\hat z$, so
that this Cartesian split coincides with the lattice partition.
\texttt{metal=True} is explicitly refused at construction time
(L.~709--717) because the small-$q$ averaging block has not yet been
adapted to it.

\paragraph{(M2) Evaluating $\Delta W^i_{\alpha\beta}$ at
$|\qpar+\Gpar|$.}  The mQEH matrix data are pre-interpolated by
\texttt{\_interpolate\_mqeh\_data} (L.~944--1039): one cubic spline
in $\omega$ moves the data onto the GW frequency grid; then a cubic
spline in $q_\parallel$ over the resulting array lets us evaluate
$\Delta W^i_{\alpha\beta}(|\qpar+\Gpar|,\omega)$ for every member of
the $\Gpar$-grid with a single \texttt{CubicSpline} call.  The result
is rescaled by $L = L_z$ at the moment it is used,
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
dW_wab = self._eval_dW_on_gwgrid(q_abs)
# dW is Hartree*Bohr^2; *= L makes x*L = 1/(N_q*2pi*A).
dW_wab *= L
\end{lstlisting}
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1156--1158).  This is the source of the
$1/L_z$ absorption discussed below \eq{Sigma-mQEH-final}.

The small-$q$ behaviour is handled exactly as in the parent class
(L.~1004--1039): inside a disk of radius
\texttt{\_q0\_cut} around $\qpar+\Gpar=0$ the spline is replaced by a
weighted average over the sampled small-$q$ points
\begin{equation}
\overline{\Delta W}^{\,q\to 0}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega) =
\sum_{q_0\le q_\text{cut}} w(q_0)\, \Delta W^i_{\alpha\beta}(q_0,\omega),
\qquad
w(q_0) \propto q_0,
\label{eq:q0-average}
\end{equation}
mirroring the integration of $W(q)$ over the $\qq=0$ disk in the
parent class.  The same logic is applied component-wise to every
matrix element of $\Delta W^i_{\alpha\beta}$.

\paragraph{(M3) Computing $\bar\rho(\Gpar;z)$.}  The pair density
\eq{pair-density} is computed by GPAW's
\texttt{ActualPairDensityCalculator} in the same call that the parent
class uses, but now with the larger PW basis.  For each unique
$\Gpar$, the implementation collects the indices of the $\Gz$ members
and applies \eq{rho-z}:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
phase_zg = exp(1j * np.outer(z_qeh, Gz_values))   # exp(+i Gz z)
n_m_gz   = n_mG[:, G_indices]                     # rho(Gpar, Gz)
rho_mz   = n_m_gz @ phase_zg.T / Lz               # bar-rho(Gpar; z)
\end{lstlisting}
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1376--1378).  Note the $1/L_z$ on the right.
The $z$-grid \texttt{z\_qeh} and the spacing \texttt{dz\_qeh} are
imported from the QEH heterostructure object together with the
density basis, so the same discretisation is used for $\bar\rho$ and
$\rho^{i}_{\alpha}$.  A self-consistency assertion guards against
unit drift,
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
assert np.isclose(self.dz_qeh, self.z_z_qeh[1] - self.z_z_qeh[0])
\end{lstlisting}
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~840--843).  The phase factors $\exp(\im\Gz z)$
depend only on the $\Gpar$ group and the q-point geometry, so they
are precomputed once per IBZ q-point and reused inside
\texttt{\_calculate\_sigma\_mqeh} (commit \texttt{fee44a26}).

\paragraph{(M4) Overlap matrix $S_{\alpha\beta}$ and overlap vector
$R^{(nm)}_{\alpha}$.}  Before entering the band loop, the rho
overlap on the het grid is precomputed at every $|\qpar+\Gpar|$:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
drho_za = self._eval_drho(qplusGpar_abs[ig])
drho_Gpar_za[ig] = drho_za
# S_{ab} = int rho_a*(z) rho_b(z) dz on the het z-grid.
S_Gpar_ab[ig] = (drho_za.conj().T @ drho_za) * self.dz_qeh
\end{lstlisting}
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1175--1181), implementing \eq{S-and-R}.  Per
band transition $m$, the overlap vector \eq{S-and-R} is built as a
single matrix product (\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1381):
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
R_m_a = (rho_mz @ drho_za.conj()) * self.dz_qeh
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{(M5) Rho-coefficients $d^{(nm)}_{\alpha}$.}
\eq{d-def} is implemented by a single linear solve
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1383):
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
d_mGpar_a[:, ig, :] = np.linalg.solve(S_Gpar_ab[ig], R_m_a.T).T
\end{lstlisting}
This is the line that distinguishes the present implementation from
an inner-product-with-$\phi$ approach: $S$ is in general
non-diagonal, and a least-squares projection is required for the
correct expansion.

\paragraph{(M6) Quadratic form and self-energy.}
The inner double sum in \eq{Sigma-mQEH-final},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}_{nm}(\qpar+\Gpar,\omega') \;=\;
\sum_{\alpha\beta}
d^{(nm)*}_{\alpha}\,
\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\,
d^{(nm)}_{\beta},
\label{eq:Tnm}
\end{equation}
is evaluated for each transition $m$ and each $\Gpar$ as a small
$N_i\!\times\!N_i$ quadratic form,
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
sigma1 += p * (d_a.conj() @ W1_ab @ d_a).imag
sigma2 += p * (d_a.conj() @ W2_ab @ d_a).imag
\end{lstlisting}
(\texttt{gwqeh.py}\,L.~1402--1403) with the same Hilbert-transformed
indexing \texttt{s*nw+w} used by the parent class.  The two terms
\texttt{sigma1}, \texttt{sigma2} are linearly interpolated in
$\omega$ to obtain $\Sigma_{n\kk}$ and similarly for
$\partial_\omega\Sigma_{n\kk}$.

\paragraph{(M7) Compatibility with the parent class.}
\texttt{GWmQEHCorrection} inherits from \texttt{GWQEHCorrection} but
\emph{severs} the legacy scalar plumbing inherited from the monopole
class (commit \texttt{95cb94b0}): \texttt{self.dW\_qw} is never set,
the parent's \texttt{get\_W\_on\_grid} is never called, and the
single \texttt{\_setup\_dW} override (L.~766--821) is the only entry
point that installs the mQEH state.  The state itself is installed by
the helper \texttt{\_install\_mqeh\_matrix} (L.~823--853), which is
the single chokepoint for the three input paths (synthetic data /
restart from NPZ / fresh QEH computation).  No scalar
\texttt{dW\_qw} is written to the restart file any more --- the
former \texttt{dW\_qwab[:,:,0,0]} slice was a misnomer since it is
the first eigenmode of $V\chi$, not the Winther--Thygesen monopole.

\section{Why the potential basis $\phi$ never appears}

A common derivation of mQEH-type formulas exploits the biorthogonality
$\int\dd z\,\phi^{i*}_{\alpha}\rho^{i}_{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$
and uses the potential basis $\phi$ to define the expansion
coefficients of the pair density.  That derivation is mathematically
clean but \emph{not applicable here}: the QEH library only ships
$\phi$ on the geometric layer width
($|z-z_i|<d_i/2$) and explicitly zeroes it elsewhere
(\texttt{qeh/buildingblock.py::Layer.get\_phi\_qaz}, L.~539--546).
The biorthogonality therefore fails on the heterostructure $z$-grid
that the GW pair densities live on,
\begin{equation}
\int_{\text{het grid}}\!\dd z\;\phi^{i*}_{\alpha}(z)\,
\rho^{i}_{\beta}(z)\;\neq\;\delta_{\alpha\beta},
\label{eq:biorth-broken}
\end{equation}
and any $\phi$-inner-product projection would silently incorporate
this truncation error.  The least-squares route via the rho basis
(\eq{d-def}) sidesteps the issue: every quantity in the projection
lives on the same het grid, on which $\{\rho_\alpha\}$ is sampled
without truncation.  The docstring of
\texttt{\_calculate\_sigma\_mqeh} (L.~1322--1326) warns explicitly
against any future change that re-introduces $\phi$.

\paragraph{Final self-energy formula.}  The expression actually
evaluated by \texttt{GWmQEHCorrection.calculate\_QEH} is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\Delta\Sigma^{i,\text{mQEH}}_{n\kk}\;=\;
\frac{1}{N_{\mathbf{q}}\,2\pi\,\Omega}
\sum_{\qq\in\text{IBZ}}\sum_{m,\,\Gpar}
\mathcal{I}_{nm}\!\Bigl[\,
L_z\,
\sum_{\alpha\beta}
d^{(nm)*}_{\alpha}\,
\Delta W^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\!\bigl(|\qpar+\Gpar|,\omega'\bigr)\,
d^{(nm)}_{\beta}
\Bigr](\eps_{n\kk}),
\;}
\label{eq:Sigma-code}
\end{equation}
with the rho-coefficients defined in \eq{d-def}.
$\mathcal{I}_{nm}[\,\cdot\,](\omega)$ stands for the
Hilbert-transformed frequency convolution implemented by the
\texttt{HilbertTransform} class and the linear interpolation in
$\omega$ along the non-uniform grid \texttt{omega\_w}.  The prefactor
$1/(N_{\mathbf{q}}\,2\pi\,\Omega)$ is exactly the \texttt{x} variable
of \texttt{\_calculate\_sigma\_mqeh}; combined with the $L_z$
absorbed in $\Delta W$ at evaluation time, it yields the
$1/(N_{\mathbf{q}}\,2\pi\,A)$ in-plane normalisation of
\eq{Sigma-mQEH-final}.

The QP correction is finally
\begin{equation}
\Delta E^{i,\text{scr,mQEH}}_{n\kk}
\;=\;Z_{n\kk}^{i}\,
\mathrm{Re}\,\Delta\Sigma^{i,\text{mQEH}}_{n\kk}(\omega=\eps_{n\kk}),
\end{equation}
identical in form to \eq{dE-Z-dSigma} of the original paper but with
$\Delta\Sigma$ now containing the full mQEH multi-pole response.  The
renormalisation factor $Z$ is taken from the freestanding-monolayer
$G_0W_0$ calculation, \texttt{gwfile}, exactly as in the parent class.

\paragraph{Note on the monopole limit.}
Setting $N_i=1$ does \emph{not} reduce \eq{Sigma-code} to the
parent's \eq{Sigma-monopole} in general.  The monopole class uses the
PW macroscopic component $\rho^{nm}(\GG=0)$ together with the QEH
matrix element $W_{(i,1)(i,1)}$ taken directly as a scalar.  The
mQEH class with $N_i=1$ uses the same scalar $\Delta W^{i}_{11}$ but
weights it by $|d^{(nm)}_{1}|^{2}$ with $d^{(nm)}_{1} = R^{(nm)}_{1}/S_{11}$,
i.e.\ the best-fit single-coefficient projection of the pair density
onto the QEH monopole density profile $\rho^{i}_{1}$ on the het
$z$-grid.  These two scalars coincide only for a specific choice of
$\rho^{i}_{1}$ (constant inside the layer, zero outside, normalised
so that the projection of $\bar\rho$ matches the layer average) ---
\emph{not} for the QEH-defined monopole eigenfunction of $V\chi$.
The two implementations are therefore consistent in form but not
numerically equivalent at $N_i=1$, and a quantitative cross-check
between them is not a regression test on either side.
